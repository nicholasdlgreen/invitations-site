# Wedding invitations — what everyone charges

Read from each site's own live calculator on 27 September 2026. Papier is carried

## How our price is built, and why it is the lowest here

Confirmed by Nicholas 27 September, and this is the rule the system follows:

    PrintedEasy list price  ×  0.80  =  our cost  =  our selling price

We buy from PrintedEasy at a fixed 20% off their published price. That discount
is passed to the customer rather than kept, because **margin is 0 on all 22
products**. So our price is deliberately **20% below our own supplier's**.

Verified against the live catalogue: 22 products at zero margin, and across
**64,151 published prices, 63,852 are exactly cost**. The other 299 sit
*fractionally below* cost — never above — by 80p to £2.40, at quantities 20, 25,
30, 50, 60, 125, 250 and 450. That is the curve-flattening doing its job:
PrintedEasy's staircase goes backwards in places, and Publish walks ours down so
we never quote more for fewer. At zero margin those 299 sell at a small real
loss, which is worth knowing but is not an error.

**An earlier version of this document said our supplier undercut us. That was
backwards and is corrected throughout.**

## What this says about setting margins

**1. There is a whole price ladder above us before we are even expensive.**
Matching PrintedEasy exactly is a **25% markup on cost** (1 ÷ 0.8), which earns a
20% gross margin and still leaves us level with instantprint. Reaching
printed.com on Gesso is a **53% markup**. Both are below the market, not above.

**2. Low quantities are where the margin has to come from.** Our A5 Silk barely
moves between 25 and 50 — £18.40 to £19.20 — because trade print is mostly a
setup charge. A customer ordering 25 pays almost the same as one ordering 50, so
a percentage margin earns very little on the small orders that a wedding shop
actually sells.

**3. Luxury stock carries margin more honestly than plain stock.** printed.com
charge 53% more than us for Gesso but only about 26% more for silk. The market
accepts a bigger premium where the paper is visibly special — and we hold four
Fedrigoni stocks.

**4. Envelopes are table stakes and we charge for them.** Free at printed.com,
forced in at instantprint, included at Papier. Ours are £7.20 per 100 A5. Quoting
card-only against their inclusive price flatters us by about 15% and reads badly
at the basket.

**5. Foiling is the biggest untapped uplift.** printed.com charge £136.96 ex VAT
for 100 foiled A5 against £51.99 plain — **+163%** — for a finish that costs us a
flat fee. Foil is also the only finish with real UK search demand.

## Traps that would corrupt this comparison

- **VAT.** Trade printers quote ex VAT, consumer sites inc VAT — a 20% swing.
  printed.com's figures are grossed up here; instantprint's are already inc.
- **PrintedEasy's calculator defaults to VAT not payable**, because UK VAT
  zero-rates leaflets. **Invitations are standard-rated.** If VAT is due on what
  we buy, every one of our costs here is 20% low. Unresolved, and it moves every
  number in this document. Accountant, not a scrape.
- **Turnaround.** instantprint quote three tiers; the cheapest is used. The same
  job on express is £37.81, not £29.29.
- **Standing discounts.** Vistaprint, Zazzle and Optimalprint nearly always have
  an offer live, so their list prices are close to fiction.
- **Price the competitor's wedding line, not their cheapest card.** Getting this
  wrong on 20 September understated four sites.
